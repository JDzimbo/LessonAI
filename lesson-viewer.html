Subject: Mathematics
Class: Basic 5
Term: First Term
Duration: 40 Minutes
Topic: Introduction to Fractions

Learning Objectives

By the end of the lesson, pupils should be able to:

  1. Define a fraction.
  2. Identify the numerator and denominator.
  3. Identify simple fractions in everyday examples.
  4. Represent fractions using diagrams.

Previous Knowledge

Pupils have basic knowledge of counting numbers and dividing objects into equal parts.

Introduction

The teacher shows the pupils an object such as an apple and divides it into equal parts. The teacher asks the pupils what happens when one part is taken from the whole.

Lesson Development

A fraction represents a part of a whole. Fractions are written using two numbers. The number at the top is called the numerator, while the number at the bottom is called the denominator.

For example, in the fraction 3/4, 3 is the numerator and 4 is the denominator.

Teacher's Activities

  • Introduces the topic.
  • Uses objects to demonstrate fractions.
  • Explains numerator and denominator.
  • Gives examples on the board.
  • Guides pupils through class activities.

Pupils' Activities

  • Listen to the explanation.
  • Answer questions.
  • Identify fractions.
  • Participate in classroom activities.

Evaluation

  1. What is a fraction?
  2. What is the numerator?
  3. What is the denominator?
  4. Identify the numerator in 2/5.
  5. Draw a diagram representing 1/2.

Conclusion

The teacher summarizes the lesson and reminds pupils that a fraction represents part of a whole.

Assignment

Write five examples of fractions and identify the numerator and denominator in each.
